2Prep: 10 minutes, before the rounds

Write these down somewhere you can copy from. You say them at the table and use them in Form 1.

  1. Your exact role. Name systems, such as enemy state machine or level layout and hitboxes, not activities.
  2. Your proof. Anything that shows you did it: a GDD page, a screen recording, a screenshot of your event sheet, a commit, the AI prompts you wrote and what you did with the answers, or notes from leading a team discussion.
  3. Your playable game link: a web export, itch.io page or Drive link that opens without signing in.
  4. One thing you explored beyond class: something you tried that we did not teach, and why you chose it.

3What to do, step by step

Find the heading that matches what you are doing right now, and follow the steps under it.

You are presenting

  1. Before the round starts, open your game and check that it runs and that the sound works.
  2. For the first 4 minutes, tell the table what has changed in your game since Week 4, what you expect from it, and anything interesting that happened while you built it, such as a bug, a surprise, or a problem you solved. Everyone has seen the game before, so do not explain what it is.
  3. For the next 8 minutes, the three players each play your game for about 2 minutes. Sit beside them and watch. Do not tell them what to do or point at things on the screen, even if they get stuck. Being stuck shows you where a new player would get stuck too.
  4. Write down where they hesitate, smile, get stuck, or where something breaks.
  5. For the next 4 minutes, listen to what they say about your game, then ask them questions. For example: What did you think the goal was? Where did you want to quit?
  6. In the last 2 minutes, close your game for the next presenter. Keep your notes, you need them for Form 1.

You are playing at the table

  1. When it is your turn, play the presenter's game. Do not ask for help. Say out loud what you think is happening, so the presenter can hear where you are confused.
  2. You get about 2 minutes. Then pass the laptop to the next player.
  3. While you wait for your turn, watch the screen and how the player reacts.
  4. In the talk at the end, say one thing you liked, one thing that confused you, and one idea.
  5. Remember how the audio sounded and how well the game played. You rate both in Form 1.

You are waiting for your turn or watching

  1. Stay at your table for all four rounds. Do not walk to another table.
  2. Watch the screen and the player's face, not your phone.
  3. Notice what you would copy and what you would avoid in your own game.
  4. Jot a line about the audio and how well it played. You rate both in Form 1.

You are an outside tester

  1. Play the game for as long as you like. The developer will only tell you its name and then stay quiet. That is on purpose, because they want to see what a new player does.
  2. Say out loud what you are thinking, including when you are confused. You cannot do anything wrong.
  3. Stop when you finish or when you want to quit. Quitting is useful information for the developer.
  4. Afterwards the developer asks you eight short questions. Answer honestly. If you rarely play games, say so, because it helps them read your answers.

7Testing your game with someone outside the class

Find at least one person outside this course and have them play your game. You run the test yourself.

  1. Pick someone who is not in this course and not on your team. Someone who plays games often gives the sharpest feedback, so choose one of those if you can.
  2. Send them your game link, or give them your laptop, together with the message below.
  3. While they play, do not help or explain. Watch the first ten seconds closely and take notes: where they hesitate, smile, get stuck, or where something breaks.
  4. When they finish or stop, ask the eight questions below.
  5. Repeat with more people if you can. You fill in Form 2 once, with one page for each tester.

Message to send your tester

Please play my game for as long as you like. I will not explain anything or help you, because I want to see what a new player does. Say out loud what you are thinking, including when you are confused. You cannot do anything wrong. When you finish or want to stop, I will ask you eight short questions.

The eight questions

  1. How much do you play games? (rarely, sometimes, often, a lot)
  2. In the first ten seconds, did you know what to do?
  3. Could you do the basic action? (move, jump, hit, collect)
  4. Did anything break or confuse you?
  5. Did the sound and effects change how it felt?
  6. Would you play again?
  7. What would you fix first?
  8. Anything else?
